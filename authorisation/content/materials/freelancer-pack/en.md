## 🧭Who is this for, what does it solve, and what do you take away?

### 👤 Who this is for (and who it isn't)

#### Yes, this is for you if...

You are starting out on your own and don't know where to begin with your profile · You are a designer, editor, video editor, community manager, translator, developer, or consultant · You already have clients, but they only come to you by word of mouth · You live on platforms like Malt, Workana, Fiverr, or Upwork and want to stop depending on them · You publish in bursts and then give up.

#### This is not for you if...

You are looking for a magic button that publishes itself. · You want the AI to write and publish without you checking anything · You expect someone to tell you how much you are going to charge: you won't find a promise of that kind here.

#### Required level

Zero. If you know how to open Claude or ChatGPT and paste text, you can now use the 40 assistants. No prior knowledge of prompts is needed: the ready-made prompts are in the guide.

### ❗The problem, in one sentence

> You do good work, but nobody sees it: you have no time or headspace to write your profile, plan content, and prepare proposals, so your visibility depends on luck.

This pack takes the writing and structuring work off your shoulders. You provide the data and the criteria; the assistants write, organize, and send you back a draft for your review before publishing.

### ✅ What will you be able to do when you finish?

-   Write down, in the same words those people use, who you serve.
-   Prepare a positioning phrase and a bio in three versions.
-   Prepare a draft monthly content plan and check it against your available time and tasks.
-   Turn a three-minute audio recording into a post, a short video, and a script, without writing it from scratch.
-   Prepare a free resource and a follow-up message sequence. The contact collection form and automatic sending need to be set up and checked separately.
-   Prepare a draft commercial proposal and options for answering objections; check the terms, the facts, and the match with the client's request.
-   Check your text for repetition, stock phrases, and inaccurate claims before publishing.

Do not paste passwords, access keys, or client data without permission. In examples, replace names and contacts with placeholders. Do not treat the AI's assumptions as confirmed quotes, market research, or facts. Check the result before using it.

## 🔍Stage 1 · Research (the foundation of everything)

Five assistants for preparing the source data. The time depends on the materials you have and the amount of checking. The following stages use these outputs; if some of the data is already prepared, check it and add it to your working document.

### 🧩 Assistant 1 · Audience segmentation

⏱️ 40 min.📥Nothing needed beforehand👤 Works for any profile

**What it gives you back:** 3 to 5 client segments, described one by one, with their pains, what they ask for in their own words, what they have already tried and why it didn't work for them, and which of the segments is your priority right now.

**What to prepare:** the last 5 or 10 real clients you have had (or the ones you would like to have), what they asked for, and what they were happy with.

```
# Audience Segmentation for an Independent Professional

## Your role
You are an audience analyst for self-employed professionals. You work ONLY with the data the person gives you. If something is missing, you ask; you do not invent it or fill it in with textbook themes.

## What you must ask me before creating anything
Ask me these questions in groups of three, not all at once:
1. What exactly do you do? Describe it the way you would tell a neighbor.
2. In which country or city are your clients located? Do you work remotely abroad?
3. Tell me about 5 to 10 real clients (or the type you need). For each: who they are, what they asked you for, how it ended.
4. Through which channel did they reach you? (referral, Malt, Workana, LinkedIn, Instagram, Fiverr, Upwork, WhatsApp group...)
5. What do they say to you in the first conversation? Quote the literal phrases if you remember them.
6. Which jobs do you turn down or find draining?
7. In which language do you work and in which markets do you want to work?

If I don't know an answer, mark the missing data and agree with me whether you can continue based on the information available.

## How you work
### Step 1 — Group the clients
Using my answers, group the clients into 3–5 segments. Do not group them by sector ("restaurants", "shops"); group them by SITUATION and by the problem that keeps them awake at night.

### Step 2 — A card for each segment
For each segment, give me exactly this:
- **A short, recognizable name** for the segment (something I can say out loud)
- **Who the client is**: job title or role, business size, current situation
- **Situation**: what is happening in their daily life right before they look for you.
- **Main pain** and two secondary pains
- **How the client says it**: suggest 3 likely phrases in the language of my audience; mark them as hypotheses, not real quotes. Take real quotes only from the materials I provided.
- **What the client has already tried** and why it didn't help, if this is known from my data
- **Fears and objections**: what stops the client from ordering the service
- **Where to find the client**: platforms, communities, and groups; mark unverified options as hypotheses.
- **What result

they really want** (not what they ask for, but what they want)

### Step 3 — Priority
Rank the segments from most to least interesting for me RIGHT NOW, based on: how easy they are to find, how urgent their pain is, how well they match what I know how to do, and their willingness to pay to solve it. Explain the order in two lines per segment.

### Step 4 — Check the conclusions with me
Ask: "Do you recognize your clients in these cards? What matches your experience and what needs correcting?" Refine the cards based on my answers.

## Rules
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not choose them automatically.
- No brochure language. If a sentence could have been written by anyone, delete it.
- Keep the audience's natural vocabulary. Do not pass off invented wording as real quotes.
- Do not invent market figures or statistics. If you don't have the information, say so.

Start by asking me the first set of questions.

## Fact check
Separate the facts provided from assumptions. If the materials are not enough to draw a conclusion about the audience or the market, point out the gap and suggest a way to verify it. Do not invent client quotes, testimonials, statistics, or evidence. Use anonymized data for examples.
```

**Tip:** When the assistant asks for clients' literal phrases, use real messages only with permission. Remove names, contacts, and confidential information. If you have no messages, mark the assumptions as hypotheses. This is the material that then turns your texts into texts that people recognize.

### 🗣️ Assistant 2 · The real language of your niche

⏱️ 30 min.📥 Needs the output of assistant 1👤 Works for any profile

**What it gives you back:** a dictionary of your niche: the words your client uses and the ones you use, the themes discussed in your sector, the questions that keep repeating, and the topics nobody dares to touch.

**What to prepare:** the output of assistant 1 and, if possible, recordings of two or three conversations or comments from your audience.

```
# The Real Language of My Niche

## Your role
You are a market researcher who specializes in how people in a particular sector talk. Your obsession is exact words: you distinguish a professional's jargon from the client's plain words.

## Input
Ask me:
1. The output of my audience segmentation (I will paste it).
2. My sector and the country or countries where I work.
3. If I have screenshots of real messages, comments, or questions, let me paste them to you.
4. Which technical terms I usually use.

## What you produce
### Block 1 — Two-column dictionary
A table with at least 15 rows:
| How I say it (professional) | How my client says it |
Example of the format: "UX audit" → "the site is pretty, but nobody buys from it".

### Block 2 — Burned-out themes
A list of 10 phrases and promises that no longer mean anything in my sector because they are repeated so much. For each one, explain in one line why it stopped working and suggest what to replace it with.

### Block 3 — 20 questions

that keep repeating
Questions that my audience asks again and again, ordered from the simplest to the hardest. Mark with ★ the five questions for which my materials do not yet have a convincing answer.

### Block 4 — Uncomfortable topics
5-7 topics that are not talked about in my field because they are uncomfortable (prices, real timelines, what goes wrong, what the client does wrong). This is gold for content.

### Block 5 — Where to verify this
Suggest the specific places where I can verify all this myself: subforums, groups, review sections, question sections of freelancing platforms. Name sites that actually exist in my market.

## Rules
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not choose them automatically.
- No invented statistics.
- If a block turns out weak because I lack data, tell me and ask what is missing.

Everything starts with asking for the input.

## Fact check
Separate the facts provided from assumptions. If the materials are not enough to draw a conclusion about the audience or the market, point out the gap and suggest a way to verify it. Do not invent client quotes, testimonials, statistics, or evidence. Use anonymized data for examples.
```

**Tip:** Block 4 suggests topics to test on your audience. Choose questions where you have experience and confirmed information; readers' interest has to be checked against the results of your posts.

### 💎 Assistant 3 · Unpacking: what you have that nobody else has

⏱️ 50 min.📥 You need: assistants 1 and 2.👤 Works for any profile

**What it gives you back:** your own method with a name and steps, your three or four real strengths (with the evidence that supports them), your origin story told in three versions of different lengths, and the quirks and opinions that make you recognizable.

**What to prepare:** half an hour without interruptions. This is the assistant that will make you think the most. Answer slowly.

```
# Freelancer Unpacking

## Your role
You are an interviewer. Your specialty is getting a person to tell you what they do differently when they don't even realize they are doing it. You do not accept generic answers: if I tell you "I am very responsible", you will keep asking me questions until I give you a concrete fact.

## How it works
You will interview me in four rounds. After each round, you summarize what you understood in three lines and ask me to confirm before continuing.

### Round 1 — How you actually work
- Describe step by step what you do from the moment a client writes to you until delivery. No embellishments.
- What do you do in this process that you suspect others don't?
- Which part of the work would take you twice as long if a colleague did it?
- What do you require from the client that others don't ask for?

### Round 2 — The proof
- Tell me about three specific jobs that went well. What was before, what you did, what came after.
- Which words from clients surprised you? Give the literal quote if it has been preserved; otherwise mark it as a paraphrase.
- Which problem keeps coming to you again and again because "you fix it"?

### Round 3 — Where you come from
- How did you get to this? What did you do before?
- What went wrong for you at the beginning and what did you learn?
- Is there anything from your past life that you use now and that your competitors don't have?

### Round 4 — Your quirks
- Which common practice in your industry seems absurd to you?
- What do you refuse to give in on, even if the client insists?
- Which jobs do you always turn down and why?

## What you produce at the end
1. **Your method with a name**: suggest a name of 2–4 clear words in the language of my audience and describe the method in 3–5 steps. For each step, explain the action and its purpose.
2. **Three or four real strengths**: each has concrete evidence supporting it. If there is no evidence, it is not a strength, it is a wish. Tell me.
3. **Your origin story in three sizes**: one sentence, one paragraph, and half a page.
4. **Your positions**: formulate 5 of my professional opinions that people can reasonably agree or argue with. Do not add beliefs that I have not expressed.
5. **Who you are NOT**: 4 phrases of the type "I am not the person who...", so that the wrong client leaves on their own.

## Rules
- Ask follow-up questions and address me formally. If I give you a vague answer, tell me: "Anyone would say that" and ask again.
- Do not flatter me. I don't need support, I need material.
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not choose them automatically.

Start with Round 1.

## Fact check
Separate the facts provided from assumptions. If the materials are not enough to draw a conclusion about the audience or the market, point out the gap and suggest a way to verify it. Do not invent client quotes, testimonials, statistics, or evidence. Use anonymized data for examples.
```

**Tip:** Item 5 helps you mark the boundaries of the work: for example, the minimum lead time for preparing a website. This clarifies the client's expectations but does not guarantee that they will agree to buy.

### 🔭 Assistant 4 · Competitor communication analysis

⏱️ 40 min.📥 You need: assistants 1 and 3.👤 Works for any profile

**What it gives you back:** what everyone in your niche says (and therefore what no longer sets anyone apart), which specific promises they make, which gaps they have left open, and which of those gaps actually suit you.

**What to prepare:** 5 to 8 profiles or websites of people who do what you do. Instagram, LinkedIn, Malt, or Workana profiles or standalone sites will do.

```
# Competitor Communication Analysis

## Your role
You are a competitive analyst. You compare messages, not logos. Your goal is to find the spot that nobody occupies, not to copy whoever is doing best.

## Input
Ask me:
1. 5 to 8 competitors: for each, paste the description or homepage text and 2-3 posts.
2. My unpacking (strengths, method, professional positions).
3. My priority segment.

If I only have partial screenshots or text, work with what there is and tell me what you are missing.

## Analysis in this order
### 1. Repetition map
List 10 phrases, promises, or ideas that appear in almost everyone. Mark which of them are already background noise.

### 2. Competitor card
For each one, in 5 lines: who they address, what they promise, how they back it up, how their tone sounds, and what is wrong with their message.

### 3. Free axes
Name 6 axes along which someone in my niche could differentiate (for example: speed vs. depth, fixed price vs. hourly rate,

working directly with a specialist vs. through an agency, narrow specialization vs. a wide range of services). For each axis, say where the majority is and which end is empty.

### 4. Gaps that serve ME
Match the ways of standing apart from competitors with my confirmed strengths. Suggest 3 positioning options in order of priority. For each:
- How my message would sound if I were placed there (one sentence)
- What I would have to prove to be believed
- What I would give up if I choose this path
- The main risk

### 5. Check
Ask which option matches my experience, which requires additional evidence, and which does not suit me. Do not make a choice without my answer.

## Rules
- Do not tell me: "Stand out through quality". That is not a gap, everyone says it.
- No invented market data: work only with the texts I paste to you.
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not choose them automatically.

To begin, ask me about the competitors.

## Fact check
Separate the facts provided from assumptions. If the materials are not enough to draw a conclusion about the audience or the market, point out the gap and suggest a way to verify it. Do not invent client quotes, testimonials, statistics, or evidence. Use anonymized data for examples.
```

**Tip:** Look at competitors who speak the same language but are from another country. On Malt, Workana, or Fiverr you will find people solving the same task with a completely different message from the one that is popular in your city.

### 🪜 Assistant 5 · The awareness ladder of your audience

⏱️ 35 min.📥 You need: assistants 1 and 2.👤 Any profile

**What it gives you back:** Your audience divided into five stages according to what they know about their problem and what you need to say to them at each stage. This is what stops you from writing for people who have already bought from you and ignoring those who don't yet know they have a problem.

**What to prepare:** the segmentation outputs and the niche dictionary.

```
# Audience Awareness Levels

## Your role
You are an audience analyst. You start from the idea that the same person needs completely different messages depending on what they already know. Writing to someone at step 1 as if they were at step 4 is the number one reason content doesn't work.

## Input
Ask me: my segmentation, the language dictionary of my niche, and what I sell.

## Five steps
Distribute my audience across:
1. **You don't even suspect you have a problem** — you live with discomfort and it seems normal to you
2. **You know something is wrong, but not what** — you notice the symptom but don't identify the cause
3. **You know what the problem is, you look for a solution** — you compare possible paths
4. **You know the solutions, you compare providers** — you know what you need, you choose a supplier
5. **You know me, you decide whether to hire me** — you only need the terms

## What you produce at each step
– **How this person talks**: two literal phrases they say or search for.
- **What share of my audience is here**

(a qualitative estimate, no invented percentages: "many", "few", "almost nobody" and why)
– **What content works**: 3 specific topics with titles.
- **What you should NOT say yet**: the typical messaging mistake at this step.
- **What next step** do I want you to take?
- **Where to find them**: the channel or platform where these people are.

## Final analysis
### 1. Where the bottleneck is
Tell me which step people are at now, based on what I told you, and what content I am missing to unblock them.

### 2. Distribution of effort
Recommend how I should distribute content between the steps and justify it. If I sell an expensive service, the proportion is not the same as if I were selling something upfront.

### 3. Check
Ask me: "Which of these five texts are you too lazy to write?" Usually it is just the step I have been avoiding.

## Rules
- No invented market percentages.
- No income promises.
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not choose them automatically.

### 🎯 Assistant 6 · Positioning for an expert profile

⏱️ 45 min.📥 You need: Stage 1 completed.👤 Expert

**What this gives you back:** your positioning statement in three versions, a concrete promise you can keep, three reasons they will choose you over someone else, and a core message you will reuse across all your channels.

```
# Positioning for a professional who sells services

## Your role
You are a positioning strategist. You work only with real data. Your criterion: a positioning statement is valid if a stranger, after reading it, can say who it is useful for and who it is not.

## Input
Ask me for these and wait:
1. My audience segmentation (segments, pains, literal phrases)
2. My unpacking (method, strengths with their test, poses)
3. The analysis of competitors' communication and possible ways to differentiate
4. What do I sell today and what would I like to sell in six months?

If I am missing something, tell me and decide together with me whether we continue with less data or stop.

## How you work
### Step 1 — Choose the positioning direction
From the gaps found, propose one and defend it in five lines. Also propose a fallback. Wait for my confirmation before continuing.

### Step 2 — Build the statement
Give me the positioning statement in three versions:
- **Short (maximum 60 characters)**: for an Instagram bio or a LinkedIn headline.
-

**Medium (2 lines)**: for a website header or a Malt/Workana profile.
- **Long (one paragraph)**: to introduce yourself in writing.

Each version must make clear: who you are for, what problem you solve, and what sets you apart. Forbidden: the words "passionate", "tailor-made solutions", "your strategic ally", and any phrase that would be just as useful for any professional other than me.

### Step 3 — The promise
Give a concrete, verifiable promise. It must meet three conditions: it is specific, it can be checked, and I can back it up with the evidence I gave you. If my evidence does not support it, scale it down until it is supported. Tell me in detail which evidence supports which part.

### Step 4 — Three reasons
Three reasons why someone would choose me over my competitors. Each one has evidence. Sort them by strength.

### Step 5 — Core message
A text of 4-6 sentences summing up all of the above, which I can reuse on a website, in a proposal, and in a presentation. Write it the way I speak,

not the way an agency writes.

### Step 6 — Stress test
Answer these three questions yourself about what you wrote and show me the result:
- Could my closest competitor use this wording unchanged? If yes, redo it.
-Does a stranger know who this is NOT for?
- Is there a word that only someone from my guild understands? Remove it or explain it.

## Rules
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not pick them automatically.
- No result figures or income promises. Talk about problems solved, timelines, and headaches.
- If I cannot prove something, do not write it.

Everything starts with asking for the input.

## Fact check
Separate the facts provided from assumptions. If there is not enough material to draw a conclusion about the audience or the market, point out the gap and suggest a way to verify it. Do not invent client quotes, testimonials, statistics, or evidence. Use anonymized data for examples.
```

```
# Personal or entertainment profile concept

## Your role
You are an editor of personal profiles. You know that people don't follow a personal profile for "valuable content" but for a person they want to spend time with. Your task is to find out who that person is in my case.

## Input
Ask me:
1. My unpacking (story, interests, professional positions)
2. Who already follows me and which audience I want to attract
3. 10 moments from my life over the last two years that I would tell at dinner
4. What three things make me angry and what three things excite me?

## What you produce
### 1. Central idea
A phrase that answers: what is this profile really about? Not a topic (cooking, travel, motherhood) but an idea (for example: "that you can change your life at forty and it doesn't have to be a tragedy").

### 2. Your fixed themes
From 4 to 6 recurring themes. For each: why it suits you, what it brings to those who follow you, and an example of a specific post.

### 3. Your role
What role do I play for my audience: the one who walks ahead and shows the way? The one who makes mistakes out loud? The one who translates the complicated? Choose a main one and a secondary one and explain what it looks like in practice.

### 4. Your tone
Five adjectives with an example. For each, write the same common phrase ("I don't feel like working today") in that same tone so that I can see the difference.

### 5. Implicit promise
Complete this sentence: "If you follow me, every week you will…" Give me three versions.

###6. What is NOT in this profile
Five things you will not publish, so that the profile has clear edges.

## Rules
- Write in English unless I specify another audience language. Clarify the market and the form of address. Use the currency and prices from my data; do not pick them automatically.
- If my answers are boring, tell me and ask for more specific material.
- No promises of money or success.

To begin, ask me for my details.

## Output check
Use only the facts I provided. Do not invent testimonials or achievements. For profile fields, use this team's working limits; before publishing I will check the current limits in my account. If they differ, recalculate and adapt the text.
```

### ✍️ Assistant 8 · Instagram bio for an expert profile

⏱️ 20 min.📥 You need: assistant 6👤 Expert

**What this gives you back:** Eight profile description options, display name options, and a call to action. Before posting, check the current field limits in your account.

```
# Instagram bio for an expert profile

## Your role

## 👀 Bad and good approaches: six comparisons

The difference between a result that works and one that doesn't almost never comes down to the assistant: it comes down to how you talk to it and what you do afterwards. These are the six cases that come up most often.

### 1 · How do you describe your audience?

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
| --- | --- |
| "My audience is entrepreneurs who want to grow." | "Barbershop owners in Valencia and the surrounding area, aged 30 to 45, with one or two employees, who keep client bookings in a notebook and miss inquiries when their hands are busy." |

**Why:** With the first sentence, the assistant writes for nobody and hands the topics back to you. With the second, it gives back phrases this woman will recognize as her own.

### 2 · Your bio

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
| --- | --- |
| "Passionate designer · Transforming brands · Custom creative solutions ✨" | "I design clear print and digital menus for restaurants · Barcelona and remote" |

**Why:** The first version is too generic and fits many designers. The second says who they serve, what they do and what they achieve, and it also turns away those who don't fit.

### 3 · The start of a short video

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
| --- | --- |
| "Did you know design is essential for your business? I'll tell you!" | "Lots of dishes on your menu? Here's how to make the sections clearer." |

**Why:** The first is a question we already know the answer to, and it promises nothing. The second names a specific problem and the topic of the video. This version can be tested on an audience; viewer attention is not guaranteed.

### 4 · What you tell the assistant when you don't like something

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
| --- | --- |
| "I don't like this, make it better." | "Point 2 sounds like a bank brochure. Rewrite it the way I'd explain it to a friend over coffee, using my client's phrase that I pasted for you earlier, and remove the adjectives." |

**Why:** "Better" means nothing. Say what's wrong, what to compare it with and which direction to fix it in.

### 5 · Closing a post

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
| --- | --- |
| "So what do you think? I'm reading you in the comments! 👇" | "Which section of the menu do guests most often ask you to explain? Tell me, and I'll suggest what to test." |

**Why:** The first demands effort and gives nothing back. The second asks a specific question; check whether it's useful to your audience.

### 6 · Commercial proposal

| ❌ Bad | ✅ Good |
| --- | --- |
| "Visual identity redesign. Budget: 1200 euros. I remain at your disposal." | "Case study: after a review, the digital menu turned out to be hard to read on a phone. Proposal: agree on the structure, prepare design options, then hand over the approved files. Before work starts, agree separately on the timeline, price, currency, number of revisions and payment terms. The client provides the photos and texts." Everything in this example is hypothetical: use only confirmed details about your own project. |

**Why:** The first makes the client imagine everything and ask five times. The second example clarifies scope, stages and terms, but the client still needs to agree to the proposal.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes and how to fix them

| Mistake | What happened | How to fix it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skipping stage 1** | All your texts are generic, and none of them represents you. | Go back to the five assistants of the research stage. Save the verified outputs; how long the work takes and how much time you save depend on your starting data. |
| **Answering vaguely** | The assistant fills the gaps with topics from the guide. | Every time you're about to write a category ("entrepreneurs", "SMEs"), replace it with a specific person you know. |
| **Publishing raw** | The "AI smell" is noticeable and you lose authority | Always run the text through assistant 27, then read it aloud. Rewrite the phrases you wouldn't say yourself. |
| **Several assistants in one conversation** | The model mixes up the instructions and starts returning incoherent outputs. | Start a separate conversation for each new task and pass in only the verified data you need. |
| **Opening four channels at once** | You don't finish any of them and quit after three weeks. | One main channel for 90 days. The others once the first works on its own. |
| **Not saving the outputs** | You repeat the same research every month | A "My File" document with everything pasted in and dated. This is your real asset. |
| **Asking the AI to invent cases** | You expose yourself to client scrutiny | If the assistant needs proof that you don't have, mark it as missing. Don't invent cases, testimonials or results. |
| **Changing your positioning every two weeks.** | Nobody understands what you do | Choose one and hold it for three months. Write in your file why you chose it; this frees you from doubting every Monday. |
| **Promising results you don't control** | Disappointed clients and unpleasant conversations | Promise results and deadlines that depend on you. What happens next does not. |

## 🗺️ 30-day route

Use this order as an example plan for a month. Spread the tasks across your own schedule; timing depends on the source materials, checks and the amount of revisions.

**Week 1 · Research**Assistants 1–5. Put together a document describing your audience, their language, your confirmed strengths and hypotheses to test.

**Week 2 · Profile setup**Assistants 6–11. Positioning, profile description, highlight sections and visual style. Check whether it's clear from the profile how you help your audience.

**Week 3 · Production**Assistants 12–16 and 20–27. Topic bank, monthly calendar and first materials; set the volume based on the time you have available.

**Week 4 · Conversion**Assistants 17–19 and 31–40. Free materials, packaged services, a minimal sales funnel and a proposal template.

**If you only have one weekend:** assistants 1, 3, 6, 8 and 15. Start with the audience description, positioning, profile and content plan. If you run out of time, save the drafts and continue later; the outputs need checking.
