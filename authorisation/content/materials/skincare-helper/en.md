## Your practical result

A clear record of your current routine, product-label facts and questions to check before changing it. The goal is a simpler decision, not an AI diagnosis or a promise of perfect skin.

A hands-on guide with a reusable prompt, a worked example and a short practice task. Use it at your own pace.

## A simple route through the task

Your actual products → Facts, observations, questions → A simpler routine record

-   What you want to understand or simplify
-   Known reactions and advice you already received
-   Budget and how many steps you can realistically follow

## The workflow, step by step

1

### Record the routine you actually follow

Write morning and evening steps as they happen now, including occasional products. The useful starting point is your real routine, not an ideal ten-step routine copied from social media.

2

### Read the labels accurately

Provide clear photos of directions and ingredient panels. Ask the assistant to show what is readable and what needs confirmation. Match each transcription to the correct bottle before comparing products.

3

### Separate observation from explanation

You can report that a product stings or feels drying. That does not establish its cause or a diagnosis. Ask AI to organise your observations and questions rather than naming a disease from a photo.

4

### Keep changes understandable

Compare product purposes and identify unnecessary duplication. Follow manufacturer directions and existing professional advice. For a new product, consult the linked dermatologist guidance on testing it first; a home test is not a guarantee or a medical allergy test.

5

### Review the record, not a promised outcome

Track dates, the products used and observations in plain language. If there is a reaction, stop experimenting and seek appropriate professional advice, especially for a severe or persistent problem. Do not ask the assistant to override treatment instructions.

## When the answer is not useful

### It diagnoses from my photo

Remove the diagnosis. Separate what is visible from what would require assessment by a clinician.

### It recommends too many purchases

Use my existing products and \[BUDGET\]. List information needed before suggesting a purchase.

### It invents ingredients

Quote the readable label text. Mark everything else as unknown.

### It suggests changing treatment

Keep my professional instructions unchanged. Turn concerns into questions for my clinician.

## Try it with your own information

Build a product table from two actual labels. Check every copied ingredient and direction, then identify one question that the labels do not answer. The exercise is complete when you have a reliable record, not when AI has selected a new treatment.
