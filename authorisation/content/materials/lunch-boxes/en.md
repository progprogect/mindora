## Your practical result

A five-day lunch plan, consolidated shopping list and two preparation sessions. Each meal includes a storage decision so a weekly plan does not become five days of refrigerated leftovers.

## The workflow, step by step

1

### Start with storage and transport

A plan depends on refrigeration, the journey to work and access to reheating. Tell the assistant what is actually available. If you cannot keep food at a suitable temperature, change the menu instead of pretending the same plan works everywhere.

2

### Build around a small ingredient set

Choose a few ingredients you like and vary their combinations. Ask the assistant to show where each ingredient is reused. Confirm quantities across all five lunches to avoid buying a full pack for every recipe.

3

### Split preparation

Schedule a second short session or suitable frozen portions. FoodSafety.gov lists many cooked leftovers at three to four days in a refrigerator at 4°C or below; the exact food matters. A Sunday-to-Friday fridge-only plan is not automatically acceptable.

4

### Check the shopping list

Separate cupboard stock, quantities to buy and optional extras. Ask for unit prices only when you supply them or current sources are available. Recalculate the total and identify packs larger than the amount used.

5

### Pack, label and review

Record preparation dates and intended use. Follow official cooling, transport and reheating guidance for your foods. At the end of the week, note which lunches travelled well and actual prep time before repeating the plan.

## When the answer is not useful

### It plans five fridge-only leftover days

Rebuild the week with a second preparation session or suitable freezing. Show preparation and intended eating dates.

### The shopping total is invented

Use only these actual unit prices: \[PRICES\]. Mark the remaining costs as unknown.

### Too many separate recipes

Use at most \[NUMBER\] core ingredients and two preparation sessions.

### The work facilities do not fit

There is no \[FRIDGE / MICROWAVE\]. Replace meals that depend on it and explain the storage decision.

## Try it with your own information

Draft a week, then audit Friday first: when was it prepared, where will it be stored, how will it travel and does it need reheating? Fix those answers before you buy ingredients. Prepare one trial lunch and check how it holds up.
