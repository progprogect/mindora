## The workflow, step by step

1

### List what is genuinely available

Check the cupboard and fridge before asking. Give quantities or say 'quantity unknown'. If you use a photo, confirm every uncertain ingredient and package label yourself.

2

### State the limits first

Include who is eating, total time, equipment, food exclusions and effort. A twenty-minute dinner must fit preparation, cooking and basic assembly, not only the time on the hob.

3

### Compare the three options

Ask which uses the fewest extra ingredients and least equipment. Reject any option that needs an unavailable appliance or ingredient before you start.

4

### Cook one chosen recipe

Ask for quantities for your group and numbered steps in order. Read the full recipe first. Check package directions, allergens and safe cooking requirements yourself; AI cannot establish whether food is safe to eat.

5

### Save the version you actually liked

Record portions, changes and actual time. Next time say 'repeat our tomato omelette, but for three people'. Keep rejected recipes separate so they are not treated as family favourites.

## When the answer is not useful

### It suggests food I do not have

Rebuild the options using only this confirmed list: \[LIST\]. Mark purchases separately.

### The recipe is too elaborate

Use one pan and no more than \[TIME\] total. Give the simplest acceptable option.

### It ignores an allergy

Do not assume a substitute is safe. List every label I need to verify before choosing.

### The amounts look wrong

Show portions and quantities for \[PEOPLE\]. Identify approximate amounts and do not invent weights from my photo.

## Try it with your own information

Plan one dinner, select one option and compare the suggested shopping list with your actual cupboards. After cooking, note the real preparation time and one change you would make. Save that tested version, not the first draft.
