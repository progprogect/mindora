## **Words into images**

**What are prompts?** You may already be familiar with AI prompts, but it's important to note the difference: for large language models like ChatGPT, a prompt is a **written instruction** you use to extract textual information or answers. For diffusion models like Midjourney, a prompt is a set of **creative commands** that guide the AI toward bringing your unique visual concept to life.

> **How is a Midjourney prompt different?**
>
> Midjourney prompts are built as **visual formulas**, not sentences. Instead of full instructions, they rely on **short descriptive phrases**, **style modifiers**, and **additional parameters** that control the look and layout of the image. LLM prompts, on the other hand, use **natural language** and **instructions** to guide textual thinking and writing.
>
> Example of a Midjourney prompt:

```
a cozy cabin in the snow, warm light glowing from windows, cinematic lighting, ultra-realistic --ar 16:9
```

> Example of a ChatGPT prompt:

```
Generate an image of a cozy cabin in the snow with warm light glowing from the windows. Use cinematic lighting, an ultra-realistic style, and a 16:9 aspect ratio.
```

Midjourney prompts also include **parameters** that are unique to Midjourney. For now, just note that you can add a parameter at the end of a prompt to control the result — for example, `--sref`, to use a style reference and keep a consistent look. We'll dive deeper into parameters and references in the next chapter, when you're ready for more control. For now, let's start with the basics.

## The anatomy of prompts

Even a simple prompt has several moving parts worth knowing about. Once you understand these elements, it becomes much easier to clearly describe your ideas and get images that truly match what you picture in your head.

### **Subject**

The **subject** is the main focus of your image, the central element that draws the viewer's eye. It can be a person, a product, an environment, or anything that defines the purpose of the scene. Whatever element sits at the heart of your image is your subject.

### **Medium**

The **medium** is the artistic format or material style your image is created in, such as photography, digital painting, watercolor, 3D rendering, or a pencil sketch.

> ![image](/guides/assets/midjourney-en/img009.gif)

### **Environment**

Although the environment can be the subject, as shown earlier, its main purpose is to serve as the background for the image — to provide additional context or even act as a storytelling element.

### **Lighting**

Lighting plays a major role in how people perceive your product. It sets the mood, draws attention to details, and gives your image personality. In Midjourney, simple cues like "soft lighting" or "bright studio light" can completely change how your product is perceived. The right lighting helps your brand look cohesive and professional.

### **Color**

Color plays a huge role in how people *perceive* a product. It sets the emotional tone before anyone even reads a word. In advertising, color can make something feel exciting, calm, luxurious, or trustworthy. In Midjourney, clearly describing your color palette helps your image connect with the audience instantly and support your brand's message.

### **Mood**

Mood sets the emotional tone of an image — it's what makes the viewer *feel* something before they've even processed the details. In advertising, mood helps align the visuals with the brand's personality, whether that's calm and reliable or bold and exciting. In Midjourney, you can shape the mood with simple cues like "dramatic," "peaceful," or "luxurious," combined with your choice of lighting, color, and style. Adding a clear mood to your prompt helps the AI capture the right emotional atmosphere, so your image connects with the audience on a deeper level.

### **Composition**

Composition is the way subjects are arranged in the frame, guiding the viewer's eye to the right place. It's what makes an image clear, expressive, and professional. In Midjourney, a few simple cues about framing and camera angle can turn a random result into a strong, brand-ready visual.

## Midjourney's approach to writing prompts

When writing prompts for Midjourney, there are a few things to keep in mind. The golden rule is that prompts should be concise and clear. Avoid using unnecessary words and phrases that only clutter the prompt. Instead of saying "Show me an image of...," get straight to the point and describe what you want to see!

> **Word choice matters!** Word choice matters because Midjourney treats your words like dials, not poetry. Similar terms steer the image in different directions, so choosing the right term gives you tighter control and fewer attempts. A quick example of "near-synonyms" with different shades of meaning: - **Soft vs. Blurry** — *Soft* = gentle edges/light; *blurry* = out of focus (loss of detail). - **Bright vs. High-contrast** — *bright* = more light overall; *high-contrast* = a bigger gap between light and shadow. - **Warm vs. Golden** — *Warm* = an overall warm color temperature; *golden* = a specific yellow-gold tint. - **Clean vs. Minimalist** — *Clean* = tidy, polished; *minimalist* = very few elements. - **Shiny vs. Reflective** — *Shiny* = a glossy surface; *reflective* = showing clear reflections of the surroundings. Use the word that *precisely* matches the look you want, and you'll get more predictable results.
>
> **Specify numbers!** Specifying numbers in your prompt helps Midjourney understand exactly how many elements you want in the frame. Without that clarity, the model often adds extra elements, such as multiple people, objects, or duplicates, because it's trying to "fill" the scene in a balanced way. A simple cue like "one person," "three bottles," or "one car" keeps the composition focused and prevents clutter or unintended repetitions.
>
> **Focus on your vision!** Midjourney works best when you describe what you want to see, not what you don't want. Positive prompts give the model clearer direction and produce cleaner, more accurate images. You can still exclude unwanted elements later using the **No** parameter, which we'll cover in another chapter.

## When does prompt length matter?

Prompt length affects how Midjourney interprets your idea. Short prompts are great for quick, focused results — they let the model fill in the creative details on its own. Longer prompts work better when you need precision, for example to match a brand style, recreate a product, or manage several elements in a single scene. The key is balance: add enough detail to clearly guide the model, but not so much that your core idea gets lost.

## Aspect ratio

**Aspect ratio** is the proportional relationship between the width and height of an image. It matters because it affects how your image displays on different screens or platforms — a wide ratio works for banners and video, while a square or vertical ratio works for social media and mobile viewing.

> ### Common aspect ratios
>
> There are quite a few aspect ratios, but below are the most common ones you'll likely use for your business or advertising projects. - **1:1 (square)** — balanced and versatile; perfect for general use and social media posts. - **2:3 (portrait)** — a taller frame, suitable for posters and portrait compositions. - **3:2 (standard photography)** — slightly wider; commonly used for natural photo layouts. - **9:16 (vertical)** — a full-screen vertical format used for Reels, Stories, and TikToks. - **16:9 (widescreen)** — the widest common ratio, great for cinematic or landscape scenes.

>
> There are several ways to change the aspect ratio of an existing image. You can use the "Pan" or "Zoom Out" feature. ### Variations

The **"Variations" (Vary)** feature in Midjourney lets you create several new versions of an image, making both minor and major changes. This is especially useful for refining a result you already like, letting you explore small but meaningful tweaks to make the image even better.

* * *

### Upscaling

**Upscaling** is the process of increasing the dimensions of an image. To simply enlarge the size without any changes, use **"Subtle Upscale."** If you want not only to increase the size but also to introduce minor creative enhancements that add an extra touch to the final image, choose **"Creative Upscale."**

> When you select the second image and upscale it, the changes may not be immediately noticeable, but pay attention to the details. As you zoom in, you'll notice that the lines become sharper and the textures finer. **This subtle increase in detail and clarity is the main benefit of upscaling.**

> Here's a GIF illustrating the difference between subtle and creative upscaling. Notice how creative upscaling introduces small but noticeable changes to the image. This happens because it doesn't just enlarge the existing pixels — it also redraws parts of the image, adding new details and improving textures, while subtle upscaling focuses on preserving the original look as accurately as possible.

**Remix** is a powerful feature that lets you generate new iterations of an existing image while editing the prompt text at the same time. ## Images into video

In Midjourney, you can animate generated images or upload your own image and turn it into a video, giving you creative control over motion and action.

From a business perspective, animating visuals into engaging moving content helps your brand stand out on social media, in ads, and in presentations, where movement draws more attention and engagement.

> 💡 **Important note:** - All plans can generate video in Fast mode, but only the Pro and Mega plans can generate video in Relax mode (and only in SD resolution). - The Standard, Pro, and Mega plans can generate video in HD resolution, but only in Fast mode. - Videos require more GPU time compared to regular images. See the "Batch size" section below for a detailed cost breakdown. - Video generation is only compatible with video-specific parameters: --motion low, --motion high, `--raw`, `--loop`, `--end`, and `--bs #`.

## Parameters

In Midjourney, **parameters** are special codes added to the end of your prompt that control the image generation process. They let you adjust settings like aspect ratio, style strength, quality, or seed values, providing greater precision and consistency in your results.

### **How to use them and common mistakes to avoid**

-   Always place parameters at the end of your prompts.
-   There must be a space between a parameter and its corresponding value.

    > Example: ✅ cozy cabin in the forest --ar 3:2 ❌ cozy cabin in the forest --ar3:2

-   Don't use other punctuation at the end of a parameter, even if there are several at the end of the prompt.

    > Example: ✅ futuristic cityscape --ar 16:9 --v 6 --style raw ❌ futuristic cityscape, --ar 16:9, --v 6, --style raw

## Permutation prompts

A **permutation prompt** lets you generate several prompt variants at once by listing different options in curly braces `{}`. Midjourney automatically creates one image for each combination, saving you time when testing product, style, or keyword variations. **Best use case for marketers and business owners:** Permutation prompts are perfect for **A/B testing visual ideas**, such as trying different product colors, backgrounds, or various elements for ad campaigns. It helps you quickly find the version that best fits your brand, without having to rewrite multiple prompts.

> ✅ **Correct format:** `a {red, blue, black} sports car parked in front of a modern building` *(Generates one image for each color variant.)* ❌ **Incorrect format:** `a {red|blue|black} sports car parked in front of a modern building` *(Vertical bars don't trigger a permutation.)* Always use **curly braces** `**{}**` and **commas** `**,**` to separate each variation. You can apply permutations to any part of your prompt, even the parameters. For example, if you want to test the same image with different aspect ratios, try using `--ar {1:1, 2:3, 3:5}`. The number of variations you can generate depends on your plan. Basic users can create up to 4 images per permutation prompt, Standard users up to 10, and Pro or Mega users up to 40. Keep in mind that permutations only work in **Fast** and **Turbo** modes — they don't work in **Relax** mode. ⛔ **Important note:** Each prompt generated with permutations uses your Fast GPU time just like a regular prompt. Since several variations are created at once, permutations can quickly burn through your GPU time, so keep an eye on your remaining balance.

## The Remix feature

The **"Remix"** feature lets you make creative changes to an image while preserving its core structure or composition. When it's enabled, you can edit the prompt or parameters each time you create a variation, giving you more control over how the image evolves. However, in practice, Remix works best for **reimagining** an image rather than for precise edits. Adding words like *"transform," "replace,"* or *"change"* can often cause Remix to fail or produce unstable results. For the most reliable results, it's better to **reuse the same prompt** or make **small changes to the wording**, such as adjusting lighting, mood, or texture, rather than issuing full-transformation commands. For precise or local changes, use the **web editor** tools.

**Best use case for marketers and business owners:** Use Remix the same way as **Retexture** or **Vary** — to explore alternative versions of a visual concept while preserving the overall composition. It's great for testing different lighting options, material finishes, or stylistic touches for a product or ad image without having to redo the scene from scratch.

> **So what's the difference between Chaos and Remix, if both tools are used to test variations?** While **Chaos** and **Remix** both help explore different image options, they work at **completely different stages** of the creative process. - **Chaos** controls the *level of randomness* during **the initial generation**. A higher `-chaos` value gives you wilder, more varied image grids from the start, helping you generate a wide range of visual ideas. - **Remix**, on the other hand, comes into play **after** you've generated an image. It lets you adjust the prompt or parameters while preserving the core composition. You refine or reimagine an existing image rather than creating a new batch from scratch. **In simple terms:** - Use **Chaos** to **explore possibilities**. - Use **Remix** to **refine and develop** a concept you already like. **Using Remix** \- ***\*Show an example of a subtle remix***\*

## Seeds

The **Seed** feature (`--seed`) controls the random starting point Midjourney uses to generate an image. Every image starts with a unique "noise pattern," and the seed defines that pattern. Using the same seed with the same prompt and parameters will recreate nearly identical compositions, making it a powerful tool for consistency and controlled experiments. **Best use case:** Seed is best used for **testing, experimentation, and fine-tuning,** rather than for everyday image creation. It lets you recreate the same starting point for your prompt, helping you study how small changes in wording or parameters affect the result. Although it can sometimes be inconsistent, it's a valuable tool for those who want to **understand Midjourney's behavior more deeply** and get a better sense of how subtle adjustments shape the final image. **Here's a great video that explains this advanced feature in more detail:** [https://youtu.be/e62RCa5bxAY](https://youtu.be/e62RCa5bxAY)

> 💡 **What to remember about the Seed feature:** **✅ Experimentation and testing:** Locking the seed is like setting up a controlled test. It gives you a fixed starting point, so you can see exactly how small changes in the prompt or parameters affect the final result. This makes seeds a great tool for structured experiments. **🚫 Not for preserving styles:** Seeds don't preserve a character, style, or brand look. They only affect the image's initial "noise pattern." For reliable visual consistency, use **Style References**, **Omni References**, or **Personalization** instead. **⚠️ Not always predictable:** Seeds can behave differently across sessions. If you try to reuse a seed after some time, the results may not match your previous images, so don't rely on it for perfect repetition. **⚠️ Not always consistent:** Even though seeds help ensure overall layout consistency, they're affected by your prompts, the model version, and your settings. Small changes to those can still produce very different results. **🚫 Not for Turbo mode:** Avoid using seeds in **Turbo mode**. Turbo prioritizes speed, and locking seeds in this mode isn't reliable, which can lead to inconsistent or unexpected results.
