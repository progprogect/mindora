## The workflow, step by step

1

### Define an acceptable trip

Write your actual constraints before searching. Include airport transfer time, arrival date, overnight stops and whether separate tickets are acceptable. Use local dates carefully when a flight crosses midnight.

2

### Collect offers at the same level

Use the same passenger count, baggage and date range. Capture the final fare breakdown where possible. Remove booking references and personal details from screenshots before sharing them.

3

### Normalise the table

Ask AI to distinguish a per-person price from the total for the group, and one-way from return. Keep unknown fees visible. A blank field must not silently become zero.

4

### Check the arithmetic and trade-offs

Add fare, required extras and transfer costs yourself. Compare practical journey time, not only flight time. Treat separate-ticket connections and overnight stops as distinct risks to investigate.

5

### Verify at checkout

Reopen the real offer and confirm names, dates, airports, bags, total price and seller terms. Check entry and transit rules with the relevant official authorities. Ask the assistant to prepare the checks; make the booking decision yourself.

## When the answer is not useful

### It invents a current bargain

Use only my supplied offers. Replace unsupported live prices or rules with 'not confirmed'.

### One option looks artificially cheap

Show whether each amount is per person, per direction or for the whole trip.

### Currencies differ

Keep original currencies. Ask me for a dated conversion rate before calculating one combined comparison.

### It ignores a practical constraint

Reject options that violate \[CONSTRAINT\], then rank the remaining offers using \[PRIORITY\].

## Try it with your own information

Collect two real offers for the same trip. Fill every comparison column, including unknowns. Recalculate both subtotals and write one reason you would choose each. Finish with the specific unanswered question that could change your choice.
